Privacy policy

Effective November 18, 2026 · Applies to Nuvori for Android, Nuvori for Wear OS, Nuvori for Windows, the Nuvori Password Fill browser extension, and this website.

The short version

  • No account, no developer server. You create a vault on your own device. There is no Nuvori account and no Nuvori server to send it to.
  • Encrypted on your devices. Your passwords, cards, codes, passkeys, notes and photos are encrypted on each of your devices. The developer cannot read them.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking. The apps and the extension collect no usage data, crash reports or advertising IDs.
  • The network only for what you turn on. Sync over your Wi-Fi, sync and backup in a hidden folder of your own Google Drive, and codes for your watch. All of it is encrypted before it leaves your device.
  • Nothing is sold or shared. The developer does not sell, rent or share your data with anyone.

1. Who we are

Nuvori is made by Santosh Kanumuri, an independent developer ("the developer", "we", "us"). This policy covers the Nuvori apps for Android, Wear OS and Windows, the Nuvori Password Fill extension for Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox, and the website at nuvori.santoshkanumuri.com.

For any privacy question, write to santosh.kanumuri@hotmail.com.

2. What Nuvori keeps on your devices

Nuvori stores what you choose to save: logins and passwords, payment cards, authenticator setup keys, passkeys, contact details for Autofill (name, email, phone and address), security questions, notes, folders, links to apps, and photos.

  • Encrypted. Vault records and photos are encrypted in the app's private storage on Android and in your Windows user profile on Windows.
  • Your master password unlocks it. The master password protects the vault key. Nuvori never stores your master password, and nobody can reset it for you. If you forget it, the vault cannot be opened.
  • Settings stay local. Appearance, Autofill choices and other settings are stored on the device.

3. What the developer receives

From the apps and the extension: nothing. They have no developer server, no Nuvori account, no advertising and no analytics. They do not send your vault, your browsing, your app use or crash reports to the developer.

The developer only receives what you send on purpose:

  • an email you write to the developer;
  • the answers you give in the tester sign-up form (see section 12);
  • reviews and ratings you post in an app store, under that store's terms.

Please never email passwords, card numbers, authenticator setup keys, backups or recovery codes.

4. Sync between your devices

Sync is optional. You can keep one vault on up to six Android phones or tablets and two Windows PCs.

  • Pairing. Devices pair on the same Wi-Fi network. You confirm a matching code on both devices before the vault is copied.
  • Changes. Paired devices send each other encrypted and signed changes straight over your local network. No server is in between.
  • Finding each other. Devices announce a random service name on the local network. It does not contain your name or your device's name.
  • When it runs. Automatic sync and "Sync when Nuvori opens" check your paired devices on the same Wi-Fi. You can turn both off.

5. Google Drive sync and backup

Both are off by default. They use your own Google account. The developer has no access to it.

The one permission Nuvori asks for

When you connect a Google account, Nuvori asks Google for access to a hidden app folder that belongs to Nuvori in that account's Drive (the drive.appdata scope). Nuvori cannot see, open or change your other Google Drive files. Requests go straight from your device to Google.

What Google can and cannot see

  • Your device encrypts every change, photo and backup before it uploads it. Sync files use a key that only your paired devices have. Backups use a key that opens only with your master password. Google and the developer cannot read your vault.
  • Google can see how many Nuvori files there are, their sizes, their times and their names. The names contain random codes, counters and times, not item names, device names or account names.
  • Google handles your Google account and these files under Google's privacy policy.

Your Google account details

Nuvori reads your Google account's email address only to show which account is connected, and stores it only on your device. On Android, Google Play services keeps the sign-in. On Windows, the sign-in token is stored inside the encrypted vault.

Nuvori does not use Google user data for advertising, does not sell it, and does not transfer it to anyone, including the developer. Nuvori's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Backup on Android

Google Drive backup keeps an encrypted copy of your vault so you can restore it on a new phone. Each phone keeps its two newest backups and deletes older ones.

Stopping and deleting

  • Choose Disconnect in Nuvori to stop Drive sync on a device, or turn backup off to stop new backups.
  • To delete the files, open Google Drive on the web, go to Settings > Manage apps, find Nuvori, and choose Delete hidden app data.
  • To remove Nuvori's access, open your Google Account, go to Security > Your connections to third-party apps & services.

6. Your Wear OS watch

The watch app shows your authenticator codes. It is optional and starts only when you choose Connect watch and sync codes on the phone.

  • The phone sends the watch an encrypted copy of your authenticator accounts (names and setup keys) through the Wear OS Data Layer. When Bluetooth is not available, the Data Layer may use Google's encrypted relay.
  • The watch keeps the codes encrypted with a key in its Android Keystore and makes codes offline.
  • The phone also sends the accent color you picked, so the watch matches. This is not secret.
  • Remove codes from watch erases them the next time the watch connects. Anyone who can unlock your watch can read the codes it holds, so keep a screen lock on it.

7. Autofill and passkeys on Android

Autofill

Autofill works only after you choose Nuvori as your Autofill service in Android Settings.

  • Nuvori looks at the structure of the form on screen (which fields are usernames, passwords, codes, cards or addresses). It does not record what you type elsewhere.
  • It fills a login, card, code or address only after you unlock Nuvori and pick one. Android passes the value to the app or website, which can then read it, as with any typed value. Nothing goes through the clipboard.
  • Nuvori saves app package names, app signing identities and website addresses with your logins, in the encrypted vault, to offer the right login next time.
  • Where Nuvori cannot confirm the website (a browser it does not recognize, a page that is not HTTPS, or a page inside another app), it can still fill. It warns you first, fills once after you confirm, and never links or saves a login for that page.
  • A card's security code fills only while you have that option turned on. If Nuvori was unlocked more than a minute earlier, it asks for your fingerprint or face first.
  • When a browser offers to save a login, Nuvori holds the submitted username and password in memory for up to two minutes while you unlock and review it. Nothing is saved until you confirm.
  • For new-account and password-change forms, Nuvori can make a strong password on your phone. It saves it in the vault before filling, so it is never lost.

Contact details without unlocking (optional)

If you turn this option on, Nuvori keeps a copy of your Autofill details entries (name, email, phone and address) on the phone, encrypted with a key in Android Keystore and excluded from backups. Your keyboard's suggestion bar and the Autofill menu can then show these values, such as your email address, before you unlock. The copy never holds passwords, cards, codes or passkeys. Turn the option off to delete it.

Suggestions for the app you just used (optional)

This is off by default. When it is on, the Vault codes picker suggests accounts linked to the app that last asked Nuvori to fill. Nuvori keeps only that app's package name, in memory, for up to five minutes. If you grant Usage access in Android Settings, the picker can also check up to five minutes of recent app activity. No app-usage history is stored or sent anywhere.

Passkeys

  • On Android 14 or newer, you can choose Nuvori as a passkey provider. Your vault must be unlocked to create or use a passkey, and you see the app or website first.
  • Passkey private keys stay in the encrypted vault. The app or website receives only a public key or a signature, never the private key.
  • Recent passkey requests in Settings lists the last apps and websites that asked Nuvori for a passkey (app name, website and whether Nuvori was offered), to help when an app doesn't show Nuvori. It is kept in memory only and is gone when Nuvori closes.
  • Passkey import uses Android's Credential Transfer. The other password manager hands the selected passkeys straight to Nuvori; no plain file is made. Nuvori keeps the passkey itself. It does not keep extra data some providers include, such as a PRF secret.

8. Nuvori for Windows

  • The vault is encrypted on your PC, in your Windows user profile. The app uses the network only for the sync described in sections 4 and 5.
  • Windows Hello is optional. Turning it on creates a Windows Hello key; Windows keeps its private key and uses it only after your PIN, face or fingerprint. Nuvori never receives your PIN or biometric data.
  • Passkeys. The Microsoft Store version can be a passkey provider in Windows 11. The private keys stay in the encrypted vault.
  • Pairing with a camera. To pair, you can scan your phone's QR code with the PC camera. Camera frames are not saved.
  • Activity list. The app shows a list of recent actions, such as "Added a card from the browser". It is kept in memory only and never contains passwords, usernames or item names.

9. The browser extension

Nuvori Password Fill for Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox works only together with Nuvori for Windows on the same PC.

What it reads

  • It runs on HTTPS pages, including frames inside them, to find sign-in, card, address and authenticator-code fields.
  • It looks at form fields and their labels to know what each field is. It does not collect or send page content, your browsing history or what you type. The one exception is a form you submit: it keeps those details, encrypted, only to offer to save them.

What it exchanges with Nuvori for Windows

The extension talks to Nuvori for Windows through the browser's native messaging, which stays on your PC. It sends the website's address (and the address of a frame with a sign-in form) and your fill or save requests. Nuvori for Windows answers with:

  • the account names saved for that website;
  • when you choose Search all logins, the names, usernames and websites of all your logins, so you can pick one;
  • for cards, each card's name, network, last four digits and color; for addresses, the saved address details, so each suggestion can show what it fills;
  • after you pick one, that one login, card, address or code. A card's security code needs your approval in Nuvori first.

The extension never receives your master password, your vault key or your vault.

What it stores in your browser

  • Details you submitted and have not yet saved or dismissed, encrypted by Nuvori for Windows, in the browser's session storage.
  • Logins you approved while Nuvori was locked, encrypted, for at most 7 days until Nuvori saves them.
  • Its settings, your "Never save" and "Don't suggest" site lists, and its last 20 diagnostic events (action, result, website host and time; never usernames or passwords).

Other details

  • The extension makes no network requests of its own.
  • Copy password puts the password on the clipboard, and the extension tries to clear it after about 30 seconds.
  • If you turn on "Use Nuvori instead of the browser's password manager", the extension asks for the browser's privacy permission and turns off the browser's own password saving and, where the browser allows it, its address and card autofill.
  • Removing the extension deletes everything it stored.

11. Data you share on purpose

  • Filling. The app or website you fill receives what you fill, under its own privacy policy.
  • Copying. A copied value goes to the clipboard. Nuvori marks secrets as sensitive and tries to clear them after 30 seconds, but your keyboard and other apps may see the clipboard.
  • Exports. A manual export is an encrypted file. If you save it to a cloud service, that service stores it under its own terms.
  • Imports. A CSV or Bitwarden export you import contains readable passwords. Nuvori does not keep a copy of the file, but the original stays where it was until you delete it.

12. This website and the tester form

  • No cookies, no analytics. The site sets no cookies and runs no analytics or advertising scripts.
  • Your theme and color choice are saved in your browser's local storage, on your device only.
  • Hosting. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log your IP address and browser details to run and protect the service; see GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Fonts. The site loads fonts from Google Fonts, so Google receives your IP address and browser details; see Google Fonts privacy.
  • Tester sign-up form. The "Join testing" form is a Google Form. It asks for your Gmail address and the platforms you want to test, so the developer can add that address to the Google Play test. The answers are stored in the developer's Google account, used only to invite you and contact you about the test, and deleted when you ask or when testing ends.
  • Store links open Google Play, the Microsoft Store, the Chrome Web Store and Firefox Add-ons, which have their own privacy policies.
  • Report an issue opens GitHub Issues. Issues are public and covered by GitHub's privacy statement, so never include passwords, codes, card numbers or vault files.

14. How long data is kept

  • Your vault stays until you delete items, reset or replace the vault, clear the app's storage, or uninstall the app. Deleting an item also deletes its photos.
  • Google Drive files stay until you delete Nuvori's hidden app data, as described in section 5.
  • Exports, imported files and pasted copies are outside the vault. Delete them yourself.
  • Deleting data does not guarantee forensic erasure from flash storage.
  • Emails and tester form answers are kept only as long as needed to answer you or run the test.

15. Your choices and rights

Your vault is on your own devices, so you control it directly: you can view, change, export and delete it at any time in the app.

For the little the developer may hold (your emails and tester form answers), you can ask to see, correct or delete it by writing to santosh.kanumuri@hotmail.com. Depending on where you live, laws such as the GDPR or the CCPA may give you further rights, including the right to complain to your data protection authority. We will answer within 30 days.

We do not sell or share personal information, as those terms are used in California law.

16. Children

Nuvori is not directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child sent us information, contact us and we will delete it.

17. Security and its limits

Nuvori encrypts your vault, blocks ordinary screenshots on Android, and locks according to your settings. No software can protect your data on a device that is already compromised, or from someone who knows your master password. Nuvori has not had an independent security audit yet.

If you find a security problem, please write to santosh.kanumuri@hotmail.com before you publish it.

18. Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, we will publish the new version on this page with a new effective date. If a change affects how your data is handled, we will also mention it in the release notes of the app version that brings it.
